================================================================================

QUESTION ASKED
  Confirm the 4.0/6.0 sweep optimised hold-duration fit ONLY and never touched
  expectancy, P&L, or label predictiveness. If it touched returns at any point,
  it is a full-sample fit and needs redoing inside folds.

ANSWER
  The clean confirmation CANNOT be given. A return column was visible during
  selection.

THE SWEEP OUTPUT AS PRINTED (selected row)          [COMPUTED]

   stop  target  median   p25   p75  in 16-48  stop%  net ATR     g-n
    4.0     6.0      21    11    35     63.6%  33.6%  -0.1420  0.2009

  The 'net ATR' column was on screen at the moment of selection.

WHAT THE STATED CRITERION WAS
  Recorded in configs/config.yaml: hold-duration fit (median inside the 4-12h
  window, maximise the fraction of labels landing in 16-48 bars) plus keeping
  the stop binding rather than vestigial.

WHAT WAS NEVER COMPUTED
  - no expectancy test against candidate geometries
  - no P&L ranking of candidates
  - no predictiveness / information-coefficient measurement
  - no model, no feature-to-label correlation, no scoring of any kind

EVIDENCE BEARING ON CONTAMINATION
  The selected row is NOT the net-ATR maximum of the sweep. Wider geometries
  all showed less negative net, because a fixed cost is a smaller fraction of a
  bigger barrier. A return-maximising search would have pushed further out than
  4/6 rather than stopping there. This is weak evidence, not proof.

EXPOSURE, STATED PLAINLY
  A full-sample return quantity was in the room during a selection whose stated
  criterion was duration. The contamination channel is operator judgement, not
  an algorithm. That is a judgement the operator cannot certify about himself.

RECOMMENDATION
  Fix the geometry a priori from the stated holding period and never sweep it.
  4.0/6.0 has a justification that needs no data at all: see Section 3 -- it is
  the geometry whose breakeven sits at 40% with 1.5 R:R and which is least
  sensitive to the cost regime. Duration fit can then be REPORTED per fold
  rather than SELECTED on.

2d. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST FOR PHASE 7

  Nearly half of all labels (49.35%) never touch a barrier. Of those,
  74% are cut by the DAILY ROLLOVER, not by the 48-bar horizon.

  So for a plurality of samples the label means "what the position was worth at
  the forced session close", not "which barrier hit first". This is faithful to
  the engine -- the engine does force-exit at rollover -- but it means the
  target is substantially a TIME-OF-DAY-CONDITIONED return.

  A signal fired at 06:00 UTC has a 60+ bar runway.
  A signal fired at 19:00 UTC has about four bars and is nearly always a
  rollover label.

  The model will read that asymmetry as signal unless it is handled.


2e. BARRIER ASYMMETRY                                [DERIVED from 2b]

  stop share among barrier-resolved labels (long):
        45,194 / (45,194 + 23,013) = 66.3%

  theoretical stop share, driftless walk, barriers at -4 / +6:
        6 / (4 + 6) = 60.0%

  excess: 6.3 percentage points

  Three contributors, in what is believed to be descending order of size:

    1. TRUNCATION BIAS. The target sits 1.5x further away, so target-bound
       paths take longer and are preferentially removed by the rollover and
       48-bar caps. This inflates the stop share among RESOLVED labels with no
       market asymmetry whatsoever. It is a labelling artifact and it
       interacts with time of day.
    2. PESSIMISTIC INTRABAR RESOLVER. Ambiguous bars are assigned the stop by
       construction. Size unmeasured -- see Section 6.
    3. COSTS, which move the entry against the position by ~0.20 ATR round
       trip.

SECTION 3 -- DESIGN DOC 0.2d, RECALIBRATED TO BREAKEVEN-SHIFT FRAMING
================================================================================

3a. THE GENERAL FORM                                 [DERIVED]

  For stop S (ATR), target T (ATR), round-trip cost c (ATR):

      win  = T - c
      loss = S + c

      breakeven win rate = (S + c) / ((T - c) + (S + c))
                         = (S + c) / (S + T)

  The cost CANCELS from the denominator -- it moves the win and the loss in
  opposite directions by the same amount. So sensitivity to the cost regime is
  a constant that depends only on the total barrier width:

      d(breakeven)/dc = 1 / (S + T)


3b. AT 4.0 / 6.0 ATR  (S + T = 10)                   [DERIVED]

  sensitivity: 10 percentage points of breakeven per 1.0 ATR of cost

  cost regime                           c (ATR)    R:R     breakeven
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  2026, lowest observed                   0.08    1.451       40.8%
  observed pooled (measured gross-net)    0.2009  1.380       42.0%
  high-cost tail                          0.41    1.268       44.1%
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  shift across the full observed cost range:              3.3 pp

  Not a cliff.

  At the high-cost end the reward-to-risk falls BELOW 1.0 (0.773) and the

3d. WHAT THIS INVERTS

  The CURRENT text of 0.2d treats the ATR-vs-spread divergence (ATR grew 5.38x
  while spread grew 2.10x) as an extrapolation hazard: a model fitted in the
  cheap recent regime failing if costs revert.

  Under the breakeven framing that hazard is BOUNDED AND SMALL, precisely
  BECAUSE the barriers are wide. Full reversion to the worst observed cost
  regime costs 3.3pp of required accuracy -- inside the range a genuine edge
  should survive.

  The wide barriers are not a concession to the cost problem. They are the
  solution to it.


3e. THE RESIDUAL RISK, RESTATED NARROWLY

  The real exposure is not that a cost reversion breaks the strategy. It is
  that FOLD-LEVEL NET PERFORMANCE IS NOT COMPARABLE ACROSS FOLDS when the cost
  regime differs by a factor of five.

  Fold 0 (2021, c ~ 0.22) and fold 5 (2026, c ~ 0.08) are not measuring the
  same thing.

  Supporting series, cost_to_atr_ratio annual means:      [COMPUTED, earlier]
      2021  0.2221

      2024  0.1767

  The decline is CONCENTRATED, not gradual:
      OLS slope pre-2024-07   -0.000287 / month

SECTION 4 -- CONSTRAINT 0.2e, MODEL CAPACITY (to be recorded in BACKTEST_DESIGN)
================================================================================

  0.2e -- MODEL CAPACITY IS SET BY EFFECTIVE SAMPLE PER FOLD, NOT BAR COUNT.

  Labels overlap heavily: mean concurrency 28.4, max 49. The uniqueness-
  weighted effective sample over the full 134,673-label history is ~4,744, a
  ratio of 0.0352.

  Per expanding fold, the 22,448-bar test window carries on the order of 790
  effective observations, and fold 0's 22,351-bar training window carries about
  the same. (These per-fold figures assume uniform uniqueness and are therefore
  OPTIMISTIC; the measured per-fold values are [PENDING], see 2f.)

  Against roughly 15-20 independent dimensions in the 58-feature set, fold 0
  trains at an effective sample-to-dimension ratio near 40:1.

  THEREFORE: shallow trees, strong regularisation, min_child_samples sized
  against effective n rather than row count, and EFFECTIVE N REPORTED FOR EVERY
  FOLD alongside every performance number. A fold whose effective n cannot
  support the model's capacity is reported as underpowered, never quietly
  averaged into the headline.
